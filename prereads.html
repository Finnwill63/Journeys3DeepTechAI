Journeys 3 · Deep Tech & AI Track
Pre-Read Packets

One packet per session. Under an hour each.

The required prep for every Tuesday — publicly available readings, videos and podcasts, held together by a short narrative. A European lean, with canonical readings from anywhere. Titles link out; each is time-boxed so the whole packet fits in under 60 minutes.

Each packet is built to finish before the session it belongs to — the session assumes you've done it. Where a video or podcast is long, a specific segment or timestamp is noted so the packet stays within the hour. Two Tuesdays have no session — 13 Oct (fall break) and 17 Nov (Slush) — so those topics are merged into the packet before each. The two checkpoints (Weeks 8 & 13) and Demo Day (Week 16) have no packet — their prep is building a synthesis.
Links were verified live at build time; if any moves, the title and author are enough to find its new home.

Part I · Foundation

Week 1Kickoff & Founder Diagnostics1 Sep

This packet is the operator mindset the program runs on — whatever you've already built, the job now is to lead on evidence and build fundability in parallel. Start with Graham on what “fundable” really means, let Altman show what investors underwrite, absorb the “do things that don't scale” ethic, then ground it in the shift into deep-tech leadership.

Essay Startup = Growth The canonical definition of “fundable”: a startup is built to grow fast, and that growth rate is the compass investors back. Paul Graham · ~18 min Video How to Succeed with a Startup What investors underwrite before revenue: a hard-to-copy idea, a market that can 10x, and a founder who ships. Sam Altman / Y Combinator · ~13 min Essay Do Things That Don't Scale The manual, unscalable moves that win real commitment — recruit users and sell by hand rather than waiting for the product to be perfect. Paul Graham · ~15 min Essay From scientist to founder: five tips for succeeding in deep tech The operating shift into deep-tech leadership — own the commercial process and the story, not just the science (Track B). IMD (European) · ~8 min
Total ≈ 54 min

Sachin Rekhi, after Rob Fitzpatrick · ~5 min Video How to Talk to Users How to run a great user interview and read the feedback without fishing for compliments. Gustaf Alströmer / Y Combinator · ~26 min Essay Milkshakes in the Morning — the Jobs-to-Be-Done story The origin JTBD case: customers “hire” a product for a job, and the real competitive set only appears once you see that job. Bob Moesta / The Re-Wired Group · ~3 min Essay The Letter of Intent Fallacy Why partner LOIs are false validation, and what genuine deep-tech customer understanding looks like instead (Track B). Alice Bentinck / Entrepreneur First (European) · ~6 min Essay How to Get Pilot Customers for a B2B Startup An 11-step framework to turn interest into a bounded, paid pilot with real success criteria and a decision date. Week 5MVP / PoC v0.129 Sep

An MVP is not a small prototype — it's the smallest thing that proves someone will commit. Ries defines it, Seibel shows how to scope it down to the one thing that proves demand, Amplitude locates the “aha” moment, and Amadeus gives Track B a proof ladder so you never confuse a demo with proof.

Essay What Is an MVP? Eric Ries Explains Ries's own definition — “maximum validated learning for the least effort” — and the warning that an MVP is not a minimal prototype. Lean Startup Co. · ~3 min Video How to Plan an MVP A tight, canonical method for scoping an MVP down to the one thing that proves demand and commitment. Michael Seibel / Y Combinator · ~11 min Essay The Aha Moment: A Guide to User Breakthroughs How to locate and design for the value moment so the MVP proves activation, not just usage. Amplitude · ~11 min Essay Deep Tech: What Proof Looks Like A six-layer proof ladder (feasibility → adoption) and the line “the fastest way to lose credibility is to confuse a demo with proof” (Track B). Amadeus Capital Partners (European) · ~11 min

Appendix · About the sources

Where these readings come from, and why they are worth trusting. Grouped by kind; European sources are marked (EU).

Foundational thinkers & operator writing

Paul Graham — Co-founder of Y Combinator; his essays are among the most-read primary texts on startups — growth, early traction, and how founders should behave.

Steve Blank — Serial entrepreneur and originator of the Customer Development method that underpins Lean Startup; a primary source on getting out of the building.

Eric Ries / Lean Startup Co. — Ries wrote The Lean Startup and popularised the MVP; Lean Startup Co. is the official home of his frameworks.

Ash Maurya / LeanStack — Creator of the Lean Canvas (a startup-adapted Business Model Canvas) and author of Running Lean.

Alberto Savoia — Former Google engineering director who coined “pretotyping” — testing that there is demand before committing to a full build.

Dave McClure — Founder of 500 Startups; creator of the “Pirate Metrics” (AARRR) funnel framework.

Andy Raskin — Strategic-narrative advisor to executive teams; his work on framing a company around a “big shift” is widely used in fundraising.

April Dunford — Positioning expert and author of Obviously Awesome; the reference on making complex products legible.

Geoff Smart / ghSMART — Co-author of Who: The A Method for Hiring; ghSMART is a leadership-advisory firm and the origin of the “scorecard” hiring approach.

Bob Moesta / The Re-Wired Group — Co-developer, with Clayton Christensen, of Jobs-to-be-Done; the Re-Wired Group popularised the “milkshake” case.

Sachin Rekhi — Product leader whose essays distil classics like The Mom Test into short, practical primers.

Startup schools & accelerators

Y Combinator — The best-known startup accelerator; its Startup Library and Startup School lectures are a free, canonical curriculum.

Seedcamp (EU) — One of Europe's earliest and most active seed funds; its “views” blog offers European-grounded founder guidance.

Entrepreneur First (EU) — A “talent investor” that builds companies from individuals before team or idea, with strong deep-tech roots.

Methods, frameworks & tools

Strategyzer — The company behind the Business Model Canvas and Testing Business Ideas (Osterwalder & Bland); a primary source for experiment design.

Learning Loop — A free, practical library of experiment “plays” — fake-door tests, letters of intent, pre-orders — for validating ideas.

Canvanizer — A free online tool for drafting Lean and Business Model canvases.

Nielsen Norman Group — The leading usability-research firm (Jakob Nielsen); the standard reference on user testing.

Amplitude — A product-analytics company whose blog is a well-regarded resource on activation and the “aha” moment.

Sales MEDDIC Group — Custodians of the MEDDIC B2B sales-qualification methodology.

forEntrepreneurs (David Skok) — A widely-cited resource on SaaS metrics and building a repeatable sales machine, from investor David Skok.

European venture capital

Index Ventures (EU) — A major European/global VC; its “Rewarding Talent” handbook and the Not Optional campaign are the reference works on employee stock options in Europe.

Point Nine (EU) — A Berlin seed fund specialising in SaaS and marketplaces; its writing on pricing and go-to-market is widely followed.

Notion Capital (EU) — A London VC focused on B2B SaaS and enterprise tech, with practical go-to-market resources.

Elaia (EU) — A French deep-tech and B2B venture firm; its writing addresses the commercialisation path of science-based startups.

Amadeus Capital Partners (EU) — A UK deep-tech VC (co-founded by Hermann Hauser) focused on hard science and what technical “proof” actually requires.

Global venture capital & operator media

a16z (Andreessen Horowitz) — A leading US venture firm whose editorial covers go-to-market, B2B, and company-building in depth.

Sequoia Capital — One of the most established global VCs; its “writing a business plan” guide is a concise template for investor-facing narrative.

First Round Review — The editorial arm of First Round Capital; long-form, practitioner-focused playbooks on building companies.

Craft Ventures (David Sacks) — Sacks (PayPal, Yammer) wrote the definitive “operating cadence” essay through his firm Craft Ventures.

Lenny's Newsletter — Lenny Rachitsky's widely-read newsletter and podcast on product, growth, and pricing.

European tech media, data & platforms

Sifted (EU) — The Financial Times-backed news outlet covering European startups; a primary window into how European investors think.

Atomico / State of European Tech (EU) — Atomico is a major European VC; its annual State of European Tech report (with Dealroom) is the reference dataset on the European ecosystem.

The Scenarionist (EU) — A media outlet focused specifically on deep-tech venture building, including corporate-partnership mechanics.

SeedLegals (EU) — A legal-tech platform for European startups; its guides cover cap tables, data rooms, and funding paperwork.

Glencoyne — An advisory focused on deep-tech technical due diligence — IP, freedom-to-operate, and technology-readiness levels.

IMD (EU) — A leading European business school (Lausanne); its innovation writing addresses the scientist-to-founder transition.

Do What Matters — A practitioner resource with step-by-step B2B guides, including how to land pilot customers.
